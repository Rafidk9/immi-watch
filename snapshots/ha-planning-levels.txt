Permanent Migration Program planning levels
On 12 May 2026, the Australian Government announced that the 2026–27 Permanent Migration Program (Permanent Program) will be set at 185,000 places, with an approximate 70:30 split between the Skilled Migration Program and the Australian Family Program. The overall planning level and the Skilled and Family split of the program are unchanged from 2025–26.
Across both the Skilled and Family programs within the Permanent Program the Government will prioritise applications from onshore migrants, allocating 129,590 places to migrants already living in Australia, and an additional 300 places allocated for Special Eligibility.
An onshore-focused program will support timely transitions to permanent residency for migrants already living, working and contributing to Australia, and further reduces the program’s impact on Net Overseas Migration (NOM).
The 2026–27 Permanent Program is comprised of:
Skilled Migration Program (132,240 places, approximately 71 per cent of the program) – this program is designed to improve the productive capacity of the economy and fill skill shortages in the labour market.
Australian Family Program (52,460 places, approximately 28 per cent of the program) – this program is predominantly made up of Partner visas. It enables Australians to reunite with family members and provide pathways to citizenship.
Special Eligibility (300 places) – provides visas for those in special circumstances. It includes permanent residents returning to Australia after a period overseas.

Skilled Migration Program
The Skilled Migration Program targets workers who have the skills, qualifications and entrepreneurship most needed in the Australian economy. It is designed to complement the skills of the existing Australian workforce and meet changing workforce needs.
Skilled migration plays an important role in filling skill shortages in priority sectors including health, ICT, engineering and science, construction and education. Skilled migration also supports development and growth in regional Australia.
The Department of Home Affairs (the department) processes existing on-hand applications and new applications in line with the Permanent Program planning levels and skilled visa processing priorities.
For more information about permanent skilled visas, see Permanent work visas.
Commonwealth Program
Skilled Independent
Increases to the planning level for the Skilled Independent category recognises this visa’s strong record of highly skilled employment and positive fiscal outcomes. For more information, see Skilled Independent.
Talent and Innovation
Decreases to the planning level for the Talent and Innovation category, recognising the intention to focus on applicants with an internationally recognised record of exceptional achievement. For more information, see Talent and Innovation.
As part of the transition to the National Innovation visa, existing applicants of the Global Talent and Distinguished Talent visa will be counted within this category. Existing Global Talent and Distinguished Talent visa applicants will not be adversely affected by the transition. Visa applicants will be assessed against the eligibility criteria at the time of their application.
You can find more information about processing priorities for this category at National Innovation visa priorities.
Employer Program
Increases to the planning level for the permanent Employer Sponsored category supports timely transitions to permanent residence for temporary migrants meeting skills gaps and contributing to the Australian labour market, see Employer Sponsored.
State and Territory Program
State and territory nominated visas
State and territory nominated visas give jurisdictions the ability to address their specific economic and labour market needs.
The state and territory nominated visas include:
the Skilled Nominated (subclass 190) visa within the State and Territory Nominated visa category
the Skilled Work Regional (provisional) (subclass 491) visa within the Regional visa category.
The Australian Government recognises the role of migration in supporting the skills and population needs of regional communities. There are a range of visa options available to encourage migrants to live and work in regional areas, including state and territory nominated places and employer sponsored arrangements. There are also concessional arrangements available under Designated Area Migration Agreements.
For state and territory nominated visas, each jurisdiction assesses applicants against their own unique criteria.

Australian Family Program
Family migration is an important element of Australia’s migration system. It allows Australian citizens and permanent residents to reunite with their family members and contribute to stronger social cohesion outcomes.
Within the Family Program, small adjustments in the Parent and Other Family categories accommodate increases to the Partner and Child planning levels, reflecting the importance of family reunion for Australians and their immediate families.
The Australian Government prioritises the reunification of a child with an Australian parent or family sponsor. This ensures we uphold our international obligations to consider the best interest of a child as a primary consideration.
Partner and Child visas are demand driven programs. Indicative planning levels are included in the overall Migration Program for planning purposes, including prioritisation of resources.
For more information about family visas, see Visa options and Family visa processing priorities.
Net overseas migration – relationship with the Permanent Program
The Permanent Program is only one component of NOM. NOM includes temporary migration, such as Working Holiday Makers and Students. NOM is calculated based on international movements in or out of Australia for 12 months or more, over a 16-month period and includes Australian citizens, New Zealanders and Humanitarian migrants.
NOM has fallen more than 40 per cent from its peak in 2023. The Permanent Program has not been a key driver of NOM in recent years, because the majority of permanent visas have been granted to migrants already living and working in Australia. In 2024–25, 61 per cent of permanent skilled visas and 39 per cent of permanent family visas were issued to people already onshore. The Australian Government has adjusted the composition for the 2026–27 Permanent Program to prioritise onshore applicants, further minimising the program’s impact on NOM and ensuring timely pathways to permanent residence for migrants living in Australia.
You can find further details about NOM in the quarterly report on National, state and territory population on the Australian Bureau of Statistics website. Details about NOM projections are at the Centre for Population’s National Projections. Further information on NOM is also available in the department’s publication of The Administration of the Immigration and Citizenship Programs Paper.
